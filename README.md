## Synopsis

In the late 2020s, while exploring the limits of relational mechanics and pulsar astrometry, Dr. Ryan Vance discovers that the classical assumptions governing space navigation—absolute Newtonian coordinate frames and continuous ground-based telemetry tracking—break down in chaotic three-body regimes. 

When an intense coronal mass ejection cripples the crewed *Prometheus* (Artemis-7) in translunar injection, conventional mission control is blinded by covariance starvation and communications blackout. Operating from an amateur workshop in the Appalachian mountains using consumer edge silicon (Nvidia Jetson Nano) constrained to 7 watts, Vance executes an emergency laser takeover. By implementing a square-root information filter (SRIF) and the relational Quantum Relational Causality (QRC) framework, he recalculates the spacecraft's state purely from onboard clock observables and saves the crew.

This breakthrough launches **Project ARGONAUT**: humanity's first fully autonomous outer-planet exploration program. From staging out of Callisto's radiation shadow to executing a daring 42-second plume dive through Europa's cryovolcanic geysers, ARGONAUT gathers physical evidence of alien biology.

Yet the mission reveals a sobering cosmological reality: the **Impedance Threshold**. Across the universe, life is abundant, but civilizations born within subterranean oceans or deep gravity wells are permanently barred by physics—the tyranny of the rocket equation and narrow chemical oxygen windows—from ever reaching the stars. Humanity's encounter with these abyssal intelligences becomes a profound ethical dilemma and the foundation for interstellar exploration.
